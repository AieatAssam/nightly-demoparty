# Appraisal, attention, and action

The context block gives each active emotion a brief private attention cue alongside its cause and possible action tendency. A cue can affect what the agent notices or considers. The situation and the available evidence still govern its interpretation and choice. The cue wording, intensity floor, and display limit are design choices.

- **Appraisal and emotional blends.** In a study using situations selected for particular appraisals, human agency and situational control differentiated anger, sadness, and guilt. The predicted differences for certainty and attention were absent in that study. Participants often reported emotional blends. This supports including cues for multiple concurrent emotions and treating appraisal mappings as context-dependent. [Ellsworth & Smith, 1988](https://repository.law.umich.edu/facarticles/1669/)
- **Risk perception.** Fear was associated with more pessimistic risk judgments and anger with more optimistic judgments. Ambiguity moderated some of these effects. In the induced-emotion study, perceived control statistically mediated the effect on optimism, while the corresponding certainty mediation was inconclusive. A fearful cue can bring possible downsides into view, and an angry cue can foreground an apparent obstacle, while the facts supporting each reading remain open to assessment. [Lerner & Keltner, 2001](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/jenniferlerner/files/lerner.fear_.jpsp_.2001.pdf)
- **Action selection.** In a human Go/No-Go experiment, reducing how much outcomes depended on participants' actions increased the influence of learned Pavlovian biases. The task supports modeling a tendency as one influence on action selection alongside learned consequences. [Dorfman & Gershman, 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-13737-7)
- **Self-talk and reappraisal.** Experiments varying the language of deliberate self-talk found that using one's name and non-first-person pronouns increased self-distancing and shifted appraisals of upcoming social stress toward challenge. Random-moment sampling separately found substantial between-person variation in the occurrence of experienced inner speech. Together these findings motivate brief private framing cues that can influence a response without requiring a verbal monologue every turn. [Kross et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24467424/) · [Heavey & Hurlburt, 2008](https://hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/heavey-hurlburt-2008.pdf)

These studies support the general mechanisms. The phrasing for individual emotions follows the engine's existing appraisal rules and remains a modeling inference.
